Redesigned Plays view · dark game-day theme

What changed

1

A matchup hero worth leading with

Your score fills the top of the screen with a win-probability bar and a live spread, so game state reads in under a second. "Close game" and "in the red zone" flags surface tension without you doing math.

2

The play feed is the star

Every scoring play is a card, not a row: position-colored avatar, the play, the NFL game line with clock, and the fantasy points earned. Touchdowns get a red rail; injuries get an amber one — glanceable event types.

3

Broadcast typography

A condensed athletic face (Oswald) carries scores, team names, and labels; a clean grotesque handles body text. Tabular figures keep every score column aligned as numbers tick up.

4

Red used with intent

The brand red now means "your points, live" — the pulse, TD rails, and your side of the win bar — instead of coating every border. Green reads as points gained, amber as risk. Semantic color, not decoration.

5

Same structure, calmer surface

Scope toggle, tabs (Plays / Mine / Opp / Top), filters, and on-deck strip all stay exactly where they are. Softer panels, one consistent radius scale, and more breathing room reduce the visual noise of a fast-moving page.

Mockup only — nothing here alters static/redzone.js or the live routes. It maps 1:1 onto the current components so the redesign can be adopted incrementally: hero card first, then the feed cards, then the type and color tokens. The app also ships a light theme; this mock shows the game-day dark treatment.
